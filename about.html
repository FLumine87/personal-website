My Experience in RoboMaster

I currently lead the algorithm team for a RoboMaster VANGUARD team. When I first joined, I had no idea how much pressure and excitement robotics competitions could bring.

One of the biggest challenges I faced was the communication between our vision system and navigation system. In the previous season, they kept conflicting. Coordinate frames did not match, and the system often froze under heavy load. I decided to rebuild the communication SDK from scratch. It took weeks of debugging and late nights, but when it finally worked, our sentry robot performed better than ever. That season, we won 5th place in the Southern Region single armour category. Seeing our robot track and hit targets reliably made all the hard work feel worthwhile.

Staying up until dawn was not always painful. The sunrises over the quiet campus, with empty streets and the faint smell of soldering iron still in the air, became oddly memorable. Watching the sky turn from dark blue to pale orange while the last bug finally disappeared from the log made the exhaustion feel like a small price to pay.

RoboMaster has been more than just a competition for me. It is where I learned how to turn theory into reality, how to fail and fix things quickly, and how to grow as both an engineer and a leader.

What I Want to Learn Next

Currently, end-to-end vision systems built on CNN are becoming the new wave. Combining this with multi-dimensional perception modules such as radar and autonomous decision-making for navigation is not only the direction of the industry but also my next goal.
